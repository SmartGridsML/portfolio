Deep RL for Maritime Route Optimisation: A Hexagonal Grid Approach

MSc thesis building a DDQN agent on an adaptive H3 hexagonal grid to navigate three Mediterranean sea routes — eliminating the geometric artefacts of rectangular grids and enabling genuine strait and canal navigation. Presented as a research poster at the Reinforcement Learning Summer School (RLSS) 2026 at ELLIS Unit Milan.

All three Mediterranean routes solved. The long-route Piraeus problem (107 BFS hops) required a BFS-seeded replay buffer to break the cold-start deadlock — without it, Q-values converge to a uniform signal and the agent never leaves its starting region.

THE PROBLEM

International shipping accounts for ~3% of global greenhouse gas emissions. The IMO's 2023 revised strategy targets net-zero by 2050. Voyage optimisation — choosing the most fuel-efficient route through dynamic, weather-varying ocean conditions — is one of the most tractable near-term levers available to fleet operators.

Classical methods (A*, Dijkstra, Tabu Search) plan once and cannot adapt mid-voyage. RL agents learn a policy that updates online as new forecasts arrive. The state-of-the-art benchmark (Latinopoulos et al., JMSE 2025) achieves 8.7–12.6% fuel savings using DDQN/DDPG on a rectangular grid — but that grid has two structural limitations this project addresses.

THE INNOVATION: HEXAGONAL GRID

  • Rectangular grids introduce a geometric artefact: diagonal steps are 41% longer than cardinal steps, forcing the Q-network to learn a geometry correction rather than navigational value.
  • H3 hexagonal grid: all 6 neighbours are equidistant — always. 6 actions, all equal cost. Q(s, "move NE") = Q(s, "move N") under equivalent progress.
  • Adaptive resolution: open ocean at res-5 (~28 km cell-to-cell), coastal zones at res-6 (~10 km), making the Strait of Bonifacio traversable and the Corinth Canal navigable end-to-end — no teleportation shortcuts.
  • Result: 10,937 water/coastal cells across the Mediterranean, with polygon-level (not centroid) land classification.

TECHNICAL APPROACH

The agent observes an 8-dimensional state vector: normalised latitude/longitude, BFS hops to goal, haversine distance, step fraction, last action bearing, progress this step, and visit count at current hex. A binary action mask prevents stepping onto land, computed once and cached per hex.

Architecture: two hidden layers (400→300 units) with LayerNorm (not BatchNorm — no batch statistics dependency) and ReLU. Double DQN with a 1M-transition replay buffer, soft target updates (τ = 0.001), and gradient clipping at L2 norm ≤ 1.0.

Reward shaping: potential-based (Ng et al., 1999) with Φ(s) = −BFS_dist/initial_dist, eliminating non-Markovian reward hacking. Without this refactoring, episode reward climbed from −773 to +165 over 1,500 episodes while success rate stayed at 0%.
